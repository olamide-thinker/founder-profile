About Olamide

I learned to code because I lost everything paying other people to build my first company.

I wasn't born into a network of wealth or given the kind of education that makes building a company easy. I also have a mind that rejects rote memorization—my brain only retains information when I am deeply excited about solving a specific, complex problem. For years, I struggled to fit into standard academic and professional molds.

My first real attempt at building a solution was an ed-tech platform. I poured all my money—and borrowed more—into it. It failed after two years because I was relying on developers who didn't care about the vision and wrote bad code. That failure forced my hand. I realized that if I wanted to control my destiny, I had to control the build.

I left, isolated myself, and taught myself how to code. Today, I am a solo technical founder. Execution risk is off the table because I build it myself.

About SyncSalez

African commerce doesn't happen on websites. It happens on WhatsApp, Instagram, and in fragmented notebooks.

While working at Polo Luxury and building e-commerce sites for years to survive, I noticed a recurring truth: the websites I built rarely generated the actual sales. The real transactions were happening on social media and third-party platforms. Yet, vendors were trying to reconcile those sales using scattered tools. They were double-entering records in Odoo and physical notebooks, struggling with constant network drops, and fighting out-of-sync online and offline inventory.

Before writing a single line of code for SyncSalez, I sat down with 50 different vendors, large and small, to separate what they wanted from what they actually needed. They needed a system that doesn't force them to change how they sell, but unifies their fragmented operations. SyncSalez blends social media, online storefronts, and offline retail into one seamless, offline-capable database on their phone.

  • Vendor

    Every seller's catalogue, staff, and storefront settings in one place.

  • Flow

    Automation for the busywork, the same category of tool as Salesforce, built for vendors instead of sales teams.

  • Collection

    A personal, intuitive database for the products, customers, and content a vendor actually works with.

  • Pipeline

    Turns real inventory into a customer pipeline, so offers go out before stock runs out.
